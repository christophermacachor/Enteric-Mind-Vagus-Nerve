## 1. Introduction: Why Epistemic Hardening Is Necessary

The MGDH proposes that scalar coherence — not vector fields — constitutes the fundamental substrate of physical reality. This is not a minor technical adjustment. It is a restoration of the quaternion scalar removed by Heaviside in 1893 [1,2], a restructuring of how we understand measurement, and a redefinition of what constitutes an observable.

Any framework with this scope will attract critique. Honest critique is welcome; it is the engine of scientific progress. But history shows that frameworks challenging entrenched formalism face a predictable pattern of mischaracterization:

- **Strawman construction:** Attributing claims the framework does not make.
- **Reduction to measurement:** Attacking measurement noise as if it disproves the theory.
- **Conflation of analogy and homology:** Citing bacterial or lower-order phenomena as disproof of mammalian-specific claims.
- **Editorial asymmetry:** Publishing critiques without rebuttal opportunity.
- **Public distortion:** Oversimplification, over-commercialization, and third-party critique masquerading as theory critique.

Stephen W. Porges, in his scholarly response to critiques of Polyvagal Theory, developed a systematic methodology for identifying and documenting these patterns [3]. This document adapts that methodology for the MGDH scalar framework. It is not a defensive wall; it is a **diagnostic lens** — a tool for evaluators to distinguish honest scientific disagreement from category error.
