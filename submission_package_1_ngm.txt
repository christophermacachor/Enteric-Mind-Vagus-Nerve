RE: Submission of Original Article
"The Scalar Convergence Theorem: A Field-Geometric Framework for Enteric 
Consciousness and Vagal Efficiency in Disorders of Gut-Brain Interaction"

We submit this manuscript for consideration as an Original Article in 
Neurogastroenterology & Motility. This work extends the foundational 
contributions of Porges (2025; doi:10.1111/nmo.14926) on vagal efficiency 
and disorders of gut-brain interaction (DGBI) into a comprehensive theoretical 
framework with direct clinical applications.

Our framework, the Macachor Scalar Convergence Theorem, proposes that the 
enteric nervous system (ENS) functions as the primary substrate for scalar 
consciousness — a biological coherence field that mediates empathy, intuition, 
and social connection through the bidirectional vagus nerve. We introduce 
eight formal theorems, ten corollaries, and a clinical research framework 
with six study designs targeting three distinct populations: intestinal 
resection patients (empathic death), GI hemorrhage survivors (empathic 
recalibration), and cardiac arrest survivors (absolute-derived motivation).

The manuscript directly builds upon Porges' recent finding that vagal 
efficiency (VE) is depressed in DGBI patients and that VE mediates 
vagal nerve stimulation efficacy. We operationalize VE as the magnitude 
response of the Aperture Transfer Function |H_p(ω)| — the quantitative 
link between enteric density detection and cardiac vagal output. We 
present five falsifiable predictions, including a VE hierarchy that 
predicts resection patients will show the lowest VE and cardiac arrest 
survivors the highest.

We believe this work is particularly suited for Neurogastroenterology & 
Motility because:
1. It directly extends Porges' DGBI framework with testable clinical 
   predictions
2. It provides the first formal theory linking ENS integrity to empathic 
   capacity through vagal efficiency
3. It addresses the 40% of GI patients with "functional" diagnoses by 
   reframing DGBI as scalar dysregulation rather than psychological 
   dysfunction
4. It includes a first-person phenomenological case study from a GI 
   hemorrhage survivor with enteric memory formation under anesthesia

The manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under 
consideration by any other journal. All authors have approved the final 
version and agree to its submission.
