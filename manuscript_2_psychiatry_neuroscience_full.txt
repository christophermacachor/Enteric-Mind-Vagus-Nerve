Abstract

The cortico-centric assumption — that consciousness, emotion, and social 
cognition originate in the cerebral cortex — has dominated neuroscience 
for a century. We present the Macachor Scalar Convergence Theorem, a 
field-geometric framework proposing that the enteric nervous system (ENS) 
— 600 million years old, containing ~2×10⁸ neurons, generating >90% of 
serotonin — is the primary substrate for scalar consciousness: a biological 
coherence field mediating empathy, intuition, and social connection. 

We formalize eight theorems and ten corollaries with empirical anchors 
in neurogastroenterology (Spencer & Hu, 2020; Greenwood-Van Meerveld et 
al., 2026), polyvagal theory (Porges, 2025a,b), and the gut-brain axis 
(Carabotti et al., 2015). We operationalize empathy as E = k × S_ens, 
where S_ens is an enteric sensitivity composite and k is the Macachor 
scalar constant. We introduce vagal efficiency (VE) as the magnitude 
response of the Aperture Transfer Function, validated by Porges' 40-year 
empirical trajectory.

We present a clinical framework with three populations: intestinal 
resection patients (predicted borderline/narcissistic phenotypes), GI 
hemorrhage survivors (predicted empathic recalibration), and cardiac 
arrest survivors (predicted absolute-derived motivation). We include 
Case Study Ω-1 — a first-person observation from a GI bleed survivor 
under anesthesia demonstrating enteric-led consciousness. We propose 
eight falsification conditions. This framework challenges the 
cortico-centric paradigm and offers a new, testable model of 
consciousness, personality, and psychiatric phenotype.

1. Introduction

1.1 The Cortico-Centric Error

Neuroscience has operated for a century under what we term the 
cortico-centric error: the assumption that consciousness, emotion, empathy, 
and social cognition originate in the cerebral cortex. This assumption is 
not empirically grounded in evolutionary biology. The enteric nervous 
system (ENS) is 600 million years old; the cerebral cortex is 250 million 
years old. The ENS contains approximately 2×10⁸ neurons (Furness, 2012; 
Spencer & Hu, 2020), operates independently of the central nervous system 
(Gershon, 1998), and generates over 90% of the body's serotonin and 
approximately 50% of its dopamine.

Porges (2025a) identifies a parallel error in psychophysiology: 
"psychophysiological parallelism" — the belief that psychological states 
have one-to-one cortical signatures — neglects the foundational role of 
brainstem and autonomic structures. We extend this critique: the 
cortico-centric error is not merely methodological bias. It is a 
categorical mistake that treats the translation layer (cortex) as the 
source (ENS) and the source as a peripheral organ.

1.2 The Scalar Framework Hypothesis

We propose that the ENS functions as the primary density well for scalar 
consciousness — a biological coherence field that mediates empathy, 
intuition, and social connection. The vagus nerve is the bidirectional 
scalar conduit. The brainstem cardiopulmonary oscillator is the central 
integration node. The cortical brain is a translation layer: necessary 
for language, narrative, and vector processing, but not the origin of 
empathy, intuition, or consciousness.

This manuscript formalizes these claims into the Macachor Scalar 
Convergence Theorem, with eight theorems, ten corollaries, a clinical

If post-surgical PTSD is enteric in origin, current treatments targeting

The mathematical formalism requires numerical simulation (reaction-diffusion
