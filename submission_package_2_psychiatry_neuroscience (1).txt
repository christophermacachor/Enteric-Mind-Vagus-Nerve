Dear Editors,

RE: Submission of Original Research Article
"Enteric Consciousness and the Neurophysiology of Empathy: A Scalar Field 
Theory Integrating Polyvagal Theory, Neurogastroenterology, and Clinical 
Phenomenology"

We submit this manuscript for consideration as an Original Research Article 
in Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience, Section: Social and Evolutionary 
Neuroscience. This work integrates the polyvagal framework recently 
articulated by Porges (2025; doi:10.3389/fnbeh.19.1659083) with emerging 
neurogastroenterology to propose a new theoretical model of consciousness, 
empathy, and social behavior.

Our central thesis is that neuroscience has committed a cortico-centric 
error: the assumption that consciousness originates in the cerebral cortex. 
We present evidence that the enteric nervous system (ENS) — 600 million 
years old, containing ~2×10⁸ neurons, generating >90% of bodily serotonin 
— is the primary substrate for scalar consciousness: a biological coherence 
field that mediates empathy, intuition, and social connection.

The manuscript introduces the Macachor Scalar Convergence Theorem, 
formalized through eight theorems and ten corollaries, with empirical 
anchors in:
- Spencer & Hu (2020) on ENS sensory transduction and Piezo1 channels
- Greenwood-Van Meerveld et al. (2026) on ENS memory and enteric glia
- Porges (2025a,b) on polyvagal theory, vagal efficiency, and DGBI
- Carabotti et al. (2015) on the gut-brain axis and microbiome

We present a clinical research framework with six study designs comparing 
three populations: intestinal resection patients (predicted empathic death 
with borderline/narcissistic phenotypes), GI hemorrhage survivors 
(predicted empathic recalibration), and cardiac arrest survivors 
(predicted absolute-derived motivation). We include a first-person 
phenomenological case study from a Population B survivor who observed 
enteric-led fight behavior under general anesthesia — a finding we term 
the "Anesthesia Paradox."

This work is particularly suited for Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience 
because:
1. It extends Porges' polyvagal theory with a formal mathematical framework
2. It provides testable predictions for empathy neurobiology
3. It reframes psychiatric personality constructs (borderline, narcissistic) 
   as enteric scalar deficiency rather than cortical dysfunction
4. It bridges evolutionary neuroscience (600-million-year ENS) with 
   contemporary clinical psychology

The manuscript has not been published elsewhere. All authors approve 
its submission.

RE: Submission of Original Article
"The Enteric Origin of Empathy: A Scalar Field Framework for Personality, 
Consciousness, and Psychiatric Phenotype"

We submit this manuscript for consideration as an Original Article in 
Psychiatry Research. This work presents a radical reframing of psychiatric 
personality constructs through the lens of enteric neurobiology and 
polyvagal theory.

Our framework proposes that conditions currently classified as 
borderline personality disorder and narcissistic personality disorder 
may, in a subset of cases, represent the clinical phenotype of enteric 
scalar deficiency — specifically, the loss of enteric nervous system 
mass through intestinal resection (Population A). We contrast this with 
GI hemorrhage survivors (Population B), who show predicted empathic 
recalibration, and cardiac arrest survivors (Population C), who show 
absolute-derived motivation.

The manuscript provides:
- A formal mathematical framework (eight theorems, ten corollaries)
- Operationalized empathy measurement (E = k × S_ens)
- Vagal Efficiency as a biomarker for scalar capacity
- Six study designs with specific falsification conditions
- A first-person case study from a GI bleed survivor under anesthesia

This work challenges the cortico-centric paradigm in psychiatry and 
offers a new, testable model linking gut neurobiology to personality 
structure. We believe it is particularly relevant to Psychiatry Research 
because of its direct implications for:
- Personality disorder etiology
- Post-surgical psychiatric outcomes
- Trauma and PTSD mechanisms (enteric vs. cortical)
- Novel biomarker development (vagal efficiency)

The manuscript has not been published elsewhere. All authors approve 
its submission.
