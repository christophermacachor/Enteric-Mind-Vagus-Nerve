1. INTRODUCTION: The Scalar Absence Across Scales
================================================================================

1.1 The 1893 Removal

In 1893, Oliver Heaviside published Electromagnetic Theory, explicitly 
rejecting the quaternionic basis of vector analysis that James Clerk 
Maxwell had used in his 1873 Treatise [1,2]. Maxwell's original equations 
preserved the quaternion norm |Q|² = w² + x² + y² + z², coupling the 
scalar potential w to the vector components (x, y, z) through algebraic 
closure [1]. Heaviside's justification was pragmatic — "engineers don't 
need quaternion philosophy" — not experimental [2]. No falsification of 
the scalar was ever offered. By Heaviside's own standard, quoting Oliver 
Lodge — "all conclusions must sooner or later be brought to the test of 
experiment" — the removal was methodologically invalid [2,3].

The consequences propagated across a century. Einstein's 1905 special 
relativity and 1915 general relativity adopted the traceless Maxwell 
tensor as fundamental, encoding T^μ_μ = 0 — the electromagnetic field 
carries no rest mass by construction [4]. Quantum field theory inherited 
this tracelessness, requiring the Higgs mechanism to import mass from an 
external scalar sector. The scalar substrate that Maxwell and Hamilton 
had treated as indivisible from the vector triad vanished from canonical 
formalism.

1.2 The Biological Parallel

A parallel absence occurred in neuroscience. The enteric nervous system 
(ENS) contains approximately 2×10⁸ neurons [5], operates independently 
of the central nervous system [6], generates >90% of bodily serotonin 
and ~50% of dopamine [5,7], and has existed for 600 million years — 
predating the vertebrate brain by hundreds of millions of years. Yet 
neuroscience treats the ENS as a digestive regulator and the cerebral 
cortex as the sole seat of consciousness [8].

Porges (2025a,b) has identified this as the "cortico-centric error" — 
the assumption that psychological states have one-to-one cortical 
signatures, neglecting the foundational role of brainstem and autonomic 
structures [8,9]. We extend this critique: the cortico-centric error is 
not merely methodological bias. It is the biological manifestation of the 
1893 scalar removal. The ENS is the primary density well for scalar 
coherence — a biological field generated by phase-locked neural and 
microbial oscillators — and its systematic dismissal mirrors the 
systematic dismissal of the quaternion scalar in physics.
